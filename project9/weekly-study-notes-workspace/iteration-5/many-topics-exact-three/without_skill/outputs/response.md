# ECON 101 Week 4: Supply, Demand & Markets

---

## 1. Demand

**Law of Demand:** When price rises, quantity demanded falls, *ceteris paribus* (all else held constant).
→ This is why the **demand curve slopes downward**.

### Determinants of Demand (these SHIFT the curve)
| Determinant | Example |
|---|---|
| **Income** | See normal vs. inferior goods below |
| **Prices of related goods** | Substitutes and complements (see below) |
| **Tastes** | A trend makes a product popular → demand rises |
| **Expectations** | Expect a price rise next month → buy now → demand rises today |
| **Number of buyers** | Population grows → demand rises |

**Related goods:**
- **Substitutes:** if the price of one goes up, demand for the other goes up (coffee ↔ tea)
- **Complements:** if the price of one goes up, demand for the other goes down (cars ↔ gasoline)

**Normal vs. Inferior Goods:**
- **Normal good:** income up → demand up
- **Inferior good:** income up → demand down (e.g., instant noodles)

---

## 2. Supply

**Law of Supply:** When price rises, quantity supplied rises.
→ The supply curve slopes **upward**.

### Determinants of Supply (these SHIFT the curve)
- **Input prices** (higher costs → supply decreases)
- **Technology** (better tech → supply increases)
- **Expectations**
- **Number of sellers**

---

## 3. ⚠️ Movement Along vs. Shift of the Curve (VERY IMPORTANT)

| | Cause | Terminology |
|---|---|---|
| **Movement along** the curve | Change in the good's **own price** | "Change in **quantity** demanded/supplied" |
| **Shift** of the curve | Change in a **determinant** | "Change in demand/supply" |

> 💡 Exam tip: if the *price of the good itself* changes, the curve does **not** shift. Pay attention to the wording: "quantity demanded" and "demand" mean different things.

---

## 4. Equilibrium

- **Equilibrium:** where **Qd = Qs**
- **Equilibrium price** is also called the **market-clearing price**

| Price is... | Result |
|---|---|
| **Above** equilibrium | **Surplus** (Qs > Qd) |
| **Below** equilibrium | **Shortage** (Qd > Qs) |

---

## 5. Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

**Formula:**
$$PED = \frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta P}$$

| Value | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| \|PED\| > 1 | **Elastic** | Quantity is very responsive to price |
| \|PED\| < 1 | **Inelastic** | Quantity isn't very responsive |
| \|PED\| = 1 | **Unit elastic** | Same % change in Q and P |

**Inelastic examples:** insulin, gasoline (short run). These are necessities with few substitutes.

### Midpoint Method
$$\%\Delta = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{(\text{new} + \text{old})/2} \times 100$$

> 💡 Why use it? You get the same answer whether price goes up or down. PED is usually negative (law of demand), which is why we compare the **absolute value**.

**Practice:** Price rises from $4 → $6, Qd falls from 120 → 80.
- %ΔQ = (80−120)/100 × 100 = **−40%**
- %ΔP = (6−4)/5 × 100 = **40%**
- PED = −40/40 = **−1** → |PED| = 1 → **unit elastic**

---

## 6. Price Controls

| Control | Definition | Example | Binding when... | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Price ceiling** | Legal **maximum** price | Rent control | Set **below** equilibrium | **Shortage** |
| **Price floor** | Legal **minimum** price | Minimum wage | Set **above** equilibrium | **Surplus** |

> 🧠 Memory trick: a **ceiling** has to be *low* to bind and a **floor** has to be *high* to bind. That's backwards from what the names suggest.

**Class Q:** *Why does a binding price ceiling cause a shortage?*
**A:** At the capped price, **Qd > Qs**. The low price leads buyers to want more while sellers offer less.

---

## 7. Surplus & Efficiency

- **Consumer Surplus (CS)** = willingness to pay − price paid
- **Producer Surplus (PS)** = price received − cost
- **Total Surplus** = CS + PS → **maximized at equilibrium**

> 💡 Price controls push the market away from equilibrium, so total surplus falls (deadweight loss).

## ✅ Self-Test Questions
1. Incomes rise and demand for bus rides falls. What kind of good is a bus ride?
2. The price of pizza rises. Does the demand curve for pizza shift? What happens instead?
3. The price of hot dogs rises. What happens to demand for hot dog buns?
4. A new technology lowers production costs. Which curve shifts, and in which direction?
5. Price falls 10% and Qd rises 25%. Is demand elastic or inelastic?
6. A minimum wage is set *below* the equilibrium wage. Does it have any effect?
7. At the equilibrium price, is there a surplus, a shortage, or neither?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Inferior good
2. No. There's a movement along the curve (quantity demanded falls)
3. Demand for buns decreases, so the curve shifts left (they're complements)
4. Supply shifts right (increases)
5. |PED| = 2.5, so elastic
6. No. It isn't binding
7. Neither, because Qd = Qs
