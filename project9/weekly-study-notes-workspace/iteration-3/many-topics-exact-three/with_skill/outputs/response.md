# ECON 101 — Week 4 Study Notes

## 1. Demand
- **Law of demand**: as price rises, quantity demanded falls, ceteris paribus.
- The demand curve slopes downward.
- Determinants of demand shift the demand curve:
  - Income
  - Prices of related goods (substitutes and complements)
  - Tastes
  - Expectations
  - Number of buyers
- Normal goods: demand rises when income rises.
- Inferior goods: demand falls when income rises (e.g. instant noodles).

**Key terms**
- **Law of demand**: as price rises, quantity demanded falls, ceteris paribus.
- **Normal good**: a good whose demand goes up when income goes up.
- **Inferior good**: a good whose demand goes down when income goes up (e.g. instant noodles).
- **Substitutes / complements**: the two kinds of related goods whose prices are a determinant of demand.

## 2. Supply
- **Law of supply**: as price rises, quantity supplied rises.
- Determinants of supply:
  - Input prices
  - Technology
  - Expectations
  - Number of sellers

**Key terms**
- **Law of supply**: when price goes up, quantity supplied goes up.
- **Determinants of supply**: input prices, technology, expectations and number of sellers.

## 3. Movement Along vs. Shift of a Curve (VERY IMPORTANT DISTINCTION)
- A **movement along** a curve is caused by a change in price.
- A **shift** of a curve is caused by a change in a determinant (e.g. income or tastes for demand, technology or input prices for supply).

**Key terms**
- **Movement along the curve**: the result of a change in price.
- **Shift of the curve**: the result of a change in a determinant.

## 4. Market Equilibrium
- Equilibrium is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (Qd = Qs).
- The equilibrium price is also called the market-clearing price.
- If the price is above equilibrium, there is a **surplus**.
- If the price is below equilibrium, there is a **shortage**.

**Key terms**
- **Equilibrium**: the point where Qd = Qs.
- **Market-clearing price**: another name for the equilibrium price.
- **Surplus**: what happens when the price is above equilibrium.
- **Shortage**: what happens when the price is below equilibrium.

## 5. Price Elasticity of Demand
- **PED = %ΔQd / %ΔP**
- |PED| > 1 means elastic, |PED| < 1 means inelastic, and |PED| = 1 means unit elastic.
- Examples of inelastic demand: insulin, and gasoline in the short run.
- **Midpoint method**: %Δ = (new − old) / ((new + old) / 2) × 100

**Key terms**
- **Price elasticity of demand (PED)**: %ΔQd divided by %ΔP.
- **Elastic**: |PED| > 1.
- **Inelastic**: |PED| < 1 (e.g. insulin, gasoline in the short run).
- **Unit elastic**: |PED| = 1.
- **Midpoint method**: working out % change as (new − old) divided by the average of new and old, × 100.

## 6. Price Controls
- **Price ceiling**: a legal maximum price (e.g. rent control). It causes a shortage if it is set below equilibrium.
- **Price floor**: a legal minimum price (e.g. minimum wage). It causes a surplus if it is set above equilibrium.
- A binding price ceiling causes a shortage because at the capped price, quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied (Qd > Qs).

**Key terms**
- **Price ceiling**: a legal maximum price, such as rent control.
- **Price floor**: a legal minimum price, such as the minimum wage.
- **Binding price ceiling**: a ceiling below equilibrium, which leads to a shortage because Qd > Qs at the capped price.

## 7. Consumer, Producer and Total Surplus
- Consumer surplus = willingness to pay − price paid.
- Producer surplus = price received − cost.
- Total surplus = CS + PS. It is maximized at equilibrium.

**Key terms**
- **Consumer surplus (CS)**: willingness to pay minus the price paid.
- **Producer surplus (PS)**: the price received minus cost.
- **Total surplus**: CS + PS, which is maximized at equilibrium.
