# ECON 101 – Week 4 Study Notes: Supply, Demand & Markets

## 1. Demand

**Law of demand:** When price rises, quantity demanded falls, *ceteris paribus* (all else equal).
→ The demand curve **slopes downward**.

**Determinants of demand** (these **shift** the whole curve):
| Determinant | Example |
|---|---|
| Income | Raise → more demand for normal goods |
| Prices of related goods | **Substitutes** (coffee ↔ tea): price of one ↑ → demand for the other ↑<br>**Complements** (printers + ink): price of one ↑ → demand for the other ↓ |
| Tastes | A trend makes a product popular |
| Expectations | Expect a price rise later → buy more now |
| Number of buyers | Bigger population → more demand |

**Normal vs. inferior goods:**
- **Normal good:** income ↑ → demand ↑
- **Inferior good:** income ↑ → demand ↓ (e.g., instant noodles)

## 2. Supply

**Law of supply:** When price rises, quantity supplied rises.
→ The supply curve **slopes upward**.

**Determinants of supply** (these **shift** the curve):
- Input prices (inputs cost more → supply ↓)
- Technology (better technology → supply ↑)
- Expectations
- Number of sellers

## 3. ⚠️ Movement along a curve vs. shift of the curve (VERY IMPORTANT)

| | Cause | Language to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Movement along** the curve | Change in the good's **own price** | "Change in **quantity** demanded/supplied" |
| **Shift** of the curve | Change in a **determinant** | "Change in demand/supply" |

**Exam trap:** A price change never shifts its own demand or supply curve.

## 4. Equilibrium

- **Equilibrium:** where **Qd = Qs**
- **Equilibrium price** is also called the **market-clearing price**
- **Price above equilibrium** → Qs > Qd → **surplus**
- **Price below equilibrium** → Qd > Qs → **shortage**

## 5. Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

**PED = %ΔQd ÷ %ΔP**

PED is usually negative because of the law of demand, so compare the **absolute value**:
| \|PED\| | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| > 1 | **Elastic** | Quantity is very responsive to price |
| < 1 | **Inelastic** | Quantity is not very responsive |
| = 1 | **Unit elastic** | Same % change in both |

**Inelastic examples:** insulin, gasoline in the short run (necessities with few substitutes)

**Midpoint method:**
%Δ = (new − old) ÷ [(new + old) / 2] × 100

*Worked example:* Price goes from $4 to $6 and Qd goes from 120 to 80.
- %ΔQd = (80 − 120) / 100 × 100 = **−40%**
- %ΔP = (6 − 4) / 5 × 100 = **+40%**
- PED = −40 / 40 = −1 → |PED| = 1 → **unit elastic**

## 6. Price Controls

| | What it is | Example | When it binds | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Price ceiling** | Legal **maximum** price | Rent control | Set **below** equilibrium | **Shortage** |
| **Price floor** | Legal **minimum** price | Minimum wage | Set **above** equilibrium | **Surplus** |

**Q from class:** Why does a binding price ceiling cause a shortage?
**A:** At the capped price, **Qd > Qs**. The low price makes buyers want more and sellers offer less.

*Tip:* A ceiling set above equilibrium, or a floor set below it, is **non-binding** and has no effect.

## 7. Surplus & Welfare

- **Consumer surplus (CS)** = willingness to pay − price paid
- **Producer surplus (PS)** = price received − cost
- **Total surplus** = CS + PS, which is **maximized at equilibrium**

## Quick Self-Test

1. Is the price of coffee rising a "change in demand" or a "change in quantity demanded" for coffee?
2. Income rises and demand for bus rides falls. What kind of good are bus rides?
3. The price of ink rises. What happens to demand for printers?
4. A new technology lowers production costs. Which curve shifts, and which way?
5. If |PED| = 0.3, is demand elastic or inelastic?
6. A minimum wage is set above the equilibrium wage. Shortage or surplus? Of what?
7. Where is total surplus maximized?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. A change in quantity demanded (a movement along the curve)
2. An inferior good
3. It falls, because printers and ink are complements
4. Supply shifts right (increases)
5. Inelastic
6. A surplus of labor (unemployment)
7. At equilibrium
</details>
