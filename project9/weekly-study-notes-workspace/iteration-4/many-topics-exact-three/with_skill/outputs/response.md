# Economics (ECON 101) — Week 4 Study Notes

## 1. Demand
- **Law of demand**: as price rises, quantity demanded falls, ceteris paribus.
- The demand curve slopes downward.
- **Determinants of demand** (these shift the demand curve):
  - Income
  - Prices of related goods (substitutes and complements)
  - Tastes
  - Expectations
  - Number of buyers
- Normal good: demand goes up when income goes up.
- Inferior good: demand goes down when income goes up (e.g. instant noodles).

**Key terms**
- **Law of demand**: as price rises, quantity demanded falls, ceteris paribus.
- **Normal good**: a good whose demand rises when income rises.
- **Inferior good**: a good whose demand falls when income rises (e.g. instant noodles).
- **Substitutes / complements**: types of related goods whose prices are determinants of demand.

## 2. Supply
- **Law of supply**: as price goes up, quantity supplied goes up.
- **Determinants of supply** (these shift the supply curve):
  - Input prices
  - Technology
  - Expectations
  - Number of sellers

**Key terms**
- **Law of supply**: as price rises, quantity supplied rises.
- **Supply determinants**: input prices, technology, expectations, and number of sellers.

## 3. Movement Along a Curve vs. Shift of a Curve
- Flagged in lecture as a **very important distinction**:
  - **Movement along the curve** is caused by a change in price.
  - **Shift of the curve** is caused by a change in a determinant.

**Key terms**
- **Movement along a curve**: a change caused by a change in price.
- **Shift of a curve**: a change caused by a change in a determinant (of demand or supply).

## 4. Market Equilibrium
- Equilibrium is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (Qd = Qs).
- The equilibrium price is also called the market-clearing price.
- If the price is **above** equilibrium, there is a **surplus**.
- If the price is **below** equilibrium, there is a **shortage**.

**Key terms**
- **Equilibrium**: the point where Qd = Qs.
- **Market-clearing price**: another name for the equilibrium price.
- **Surplus**: occurs when price is above equilibrium.
- **Shortage**: occurs when price is below equilibrium.

## 5. Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)
- Formula: **PED = %ΔQd / %ΔP**
- Interpreting the value:
  - |PED| > 1: elastic
  - |PED| < 1: inelastic
  - |PED| = 1: unit elastic
- Examples of inelastic demand: insulin, and gasoline in the short run.
- **Midpoint method** for percentage change:
  %Δ = (new − old) / ((new + old) / 2) × 100

**Key terms**
- **Price elasticity of demand (PED)**: %ΔQd / %ΔP.
- **Elastic**: |PED| > 1.
- **Inelastic**: |PED| < 1 (e.g. insulin, short-run gasoline).
- **Unit elastic**: |PED| = 1.
- **Midpoint method**: %Δ = (new − old) / ((new + old) / 2) × 100.

## 6. Price Controls
- **Price ceiling**: a legal maximum price (e.g. rent control). It causes a shortage if set below equilibrium.
- **Price floor**: a legal minimum price (e.g. minimum wage). It causes a surplus if set above equilibrium.
- A binding price ceiling causes a shortage because, at the capped price, quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied (Qd > Qs).

**Key terms**
- **Price ceiling**: a legal maximum price, such as rent control.
- **Price floor**: a legal minimum price, such as minimum wage.

## 7. Consumer and Producer Surplus
- **Consumer surplus (CS)** = willingness to pay − price paid.
- **Producer surplus (PS)** = price received − cost.
- **Total surplus** = CS + PS, and it is maximized at equilibrium.

**Key terms**
- **Consumer surplus**: willingness to pay minus price paid.
- **Producer surplus**: price received minus cost.
- **Total surplus**: CS + PS. It is maximized at equilibrium.
