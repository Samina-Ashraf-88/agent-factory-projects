# HIST 210 — Week 5 Study Notes

## 1. Causes of the French Revolution
- The French Revolution started in 1789.
- **⚠️ Your professor said the causes will definitely be on the midterm.**
- Causes:
  - **Financial crisis**: debt from wars, including the American War of Independence.
  - **Bad harvests**: these led to high bread prices.
  - **Enlightenment ideas**: e.g. Rousseau and Montesquieu.

**Key terms**
- **Financial crisis**: a cause of the Revolution, driven by debt from wars, including the American War of Independence.
- **Bad harvests**: a cause of the Revolution that led to high bread prices.
- **Enlightenment ideas**: a cause of the Revolution, associated with thinkers such as Rousseau and Montesquieu.

## 2. The Estates General and the National Assembly
- The Estates General met in May 1789, the first time it had met since 1614.
- There were three estates:
  - First Estate: clergy
  - Second Estate: nobility
  - Third Estate: everyone else (about 97% of the population)
- The Third Estate broke off and formed the National Assembly.
- Tennis Court Oath: 20 June 1789.

**Key terms**
- **Estates General**: the assembly of the three estates. It met in May 1789 for the first time since 1614.
- **Third Estate**: everyone outside the clergy and nobility, about 97% of the population.
- **National Assembly**: the body formed when the Third Estate broke off from the Estates General.
- **Tennis Court Oath**: 20 June 1789, linked to the formation of the National Assembly.

## 3. Storming of the Bastille and the Declaration of Rights
- Storming of the Bastille: 14 July 1789. This date is now the French national day.
- Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen: August 1789. Its principles were liberty, equality and popular sovereignty.

**Key terms**
- **Storming of the Bastille**: event of 14 July 1789, now commemorated as the French national day.
- **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen**: August 1789 declaration of liberty, equality and popular sovereignty.

## 4. The Reign of Terror
- The Reign of Terror lasted from 1793 to 1794.
- Key figures and bodies were Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety.
- There were about 17,000 official executions.
- Robespierre was executed in July 1794 (Thermidor).

**Key terms**
- **Reign of Terror**: the period from 1793 to 1794 associated with Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety, with about 17,000 official executions.
- **Robespierre**: a leading figure of the Reign of Terror, executed in July 1794.
- **Thermidor**: the name your notes give to Robespierre's execution in July 1794.

## 5. End of the Revolution
- Napoleon's coup in 1799 is usually treated as the end of the Revolution.

**Key terms**
- **Napoleon's coup (1799)**: the event usually seen as marking the end of the Revolution.
