# ECON 101: Week 4 Study Notes
*Supply, Demand, Elasticity & Price Controls*

---

## 1. Demand

**Law of Demand:** When price rises, quantity demanded falls, *ceteris paribus* (all else held constant).
→ This is why the **demand curve slopes downward**. ⭐

**Determinants of demand (these SHIFT the curve):**
| Determinant | Example |
|---|---|
| Income | A raise means you buy more restaurant meals |
| Prices of related goods | **Substitutes** (Coke/Pepsi) and **complements** (printers/ink) |
| Tastes | A trend makes a product popular |
| Expectations | You expect prices to rise next month, so you buy now |
| Number of buyers | Population growth |

**Normal vs. inferior goods:**
- **Normal good:** income ↑ → demand ↑
- **Inferior good:** income ↑ → demand ↓ (e.g., instant noodles)

---

## 2. Supply

**Law of Supply:** When price rises, quantity supplied rises (ceteris paribus).
→ The supply curve slopes **upward**.

**Determinants of supply (these SHIFT the curve):**
- Input prices
- Technology
- Expectations
- Number of sellers

---

## 3. ⚠️ Movement vs. Shift (VERY IMPORTANT)

| | Cause | Terminology |
|---|---|---|
| **Movement along** the curve | A change in the good's **own price** | "Change in **quantity** demanded/supplied" |
| **Shift of** the curve | A change in a **determinant** | "Change in demand/supply" |

> 💡 Exam trap: "A rise in the price of coffee decreases the *demand* for coffee." This is **wrong**. It decreases the *quantity demanded*, which is a movement along the curve.

---

## 4. Equilibrium

- **Equilibrium:** where **Qd = Qs**
- Equilibrium price is also called the **market-clearing price**

| Price is... | Result |
|---|---|
| **Above** equilibrium | **Surplus** (Qs > Qd) |
| **Below** equilibrium | **Shortage** (Qd > Qs) |

---

## 5. Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

**Formula:**
$$PED = \frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta P}$$

Use the **absolute value** to classify. PED is normally negative because of the law of demand.

| \|PED\| | Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| > 1 | **Elastic** | Quantity is very responsive to price |
| < 1 | **Inelastic** | Quantity is not very responsive to price |
| = 1 | **Unit elastic** | Proportional response |

**Inelastic examples:** insulin, gasoline (in the short run)

### Midpoint Method
$$\%\Delta = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{(\text{new} + \text{old})/2} \times 100$$

**Worked example:** Price rises from $4 to $6, and Qd falls from 120 to 80.
- %ΔQ = (80 − 120) / 100 × 100 = **−40%**
- %ΔP = (6 − 4) / 5 × 100 = **+40%**
- PED = −40 / 40 = **−1**, so |PED| = 1 and demand is **unit elastic**

---

## 6. Price Controls

| | Definition | Example | Binding when... | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Price ceiling** | Legal **maximum** price | Rent control | Set **below** equilibrium | **Shortage** |
| **Price floor** | Legal **minimum** price | Minimum wage | Set **above** equilibrium | **Surplus** |

> 💡 Memory trick: a ceiling has to be *low* to matter, and a floor has to be *high* to matter.

**Class question:** *Why does a binding price ceiling cause a shortage?*
**Answer:** At the capped price, **Qd > Qs**. The low price makes buyers want more while sellers offer less.

---

## 7. Surplus & Welfare

- **Consumer surplus (CS)** = willingness to pay − price paid
- **Producer surplus (PS)** = price received − cost
- **Total surplus** = CS + PS, which is **maximized at equilibrium**

## ✅ Self-Test Questions

1. Incomes rise and demand for bus rides falls. What kind of good is a bus ride?
2. A new technology lowers production costs. Is that a movement along the supply curve or a shift? Which direction?
3. The price of hot dogs rises. What happens to demand for hot dog buns, and why?
4. The price is $10 and the equilibrium price is $8. Is there a surplus or a shortage?
5. Price falls from $10 to $8 and Qd rises from 50 to 70. Use the midpoint method to calculate PED. Is demand elastic or inelastic?
6. The government sets a price ceiling *above* equilibrium. What happens?
7. Your willingness to pay is $50 and you pay $35. What is your consumer surplus?

<details>
<summary><b>Answers</b></summary>

1. Inferior good
2. A shift to the right (supply increases)
3. Demand for buns falls (the curve shifts left) because hot dogs and buns are complements
4. A surplus, because the price is above equilibrium
5. %ΔQ = 20/60 = 33.3%, %ΔP = −2/9 = −22.2%, so |PED| ≈ 1.5 and demand is **elastic**
6. Nothing. The ceiling is non-binding, so the market stays at equilibrium.
7. $15

</details>
