# ECON 101 — Week 4 Study Notes

## 1. Demand
- **Law of demand**: as price rises, quantity demanded falls, *ceteris paribus*.
- The demand curve slopes **downward**. Your notes flag this twice, so expect it on the exam.
- **Determinants of demand** (these shift the demand curve):
  - Income
  - Prices of related goods (substitutes and complements)
  - Tastes
  - Expectations
  - Number of buyers
- **Normal good**: demand goes up when income goes up.
- **Inferior good**: demand goes down when income goes up (e.g. instant noodles).

**Key terms**
- **Law of demand**: as price rises, quantity demanded falls, ceteris paribus.
- **Normal good**: a good whose demand rises when income rises.
- **Inferior good**: a good whose demand falls when income rises (e.g. instant noodles).
- **Determinants of demand**: income, prices of related goods (substitutes, complements), tastes, expectations, and number of buyers. Changes in these shift the demand curve.

## 2. Supply
- **Law of supply**: as price goes up, quantity supplied goes up.
- **Determinants of supply** (these shift the supply curve):
  - Input prices
  - Technology
  - Expectations
  - Number of sellers

**Key terms**
- **Law of supply**: as price rises, quantity supplied rises.
- **Determinants of supply**: input prices, technology, expectations, and number of sellers.

## 3. Movement Along a Curve vs. Shift of a Curve
- **Movement along a curve** is caused by a change in **price**.
- **Shift of a curve** is caused by a change in a **determinant**.
- Your notes mark this as a **VERY IMPORTANT DISTINCTION**.

**Key terms**
- **Movement along the curve**: a change caused by a change in price.
- **Shift of the curve**: a change caused by a change in a determinant of demand or supply.

## 4. Equilibrium
- **Equilibrium** is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (Qd = Qs).
- The equilibrium price is also called the **market-clearing price**.
- **Surplus**: occurs when the price is **above** equilibrium.
- **Shortage**: occurs when the price is **below** equilibrium.

**Key terms**
- **Equilibrium**: the point where Qd = Qs.
- **Market-clearing price**: another name for the equilibrium price.
- **Surplus**: the result when price is above equilibrium.
- **Shortage**: the result when price is below equilibrium.

## 5. Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)
- **Formula**: PED = %ΔQd / %ΔP
- **Interpreting PED**:
  - |PED| > 1 → **elastic**
  - |PED| < 1 → **inelastic**
  - |PED| = 1 → **unit elastic**
- **Examples of inelastic demand**: insulin, and gasoline in the short run.
- **Midpoint method** for percentage change:
  - %Δ = (new − old) / ((new + old) / 2) × 100

**Key terms**
- **Price elasticity of demand (PED)**: %ΔQd / %ΔP.
- **Elastic**: |PED| > 1.
- **Inelastic**: |PED| < 1 (e.g. insulin, gasoline in the short run).
- **Unit elastic**: |PED| = 1.
- **Midpoint method**: %Δ = (new − old) / ((new + old) / 2) × 100.

## 6. Price Controls
- **Price ceiling**: a legal maximum price (e.g. rent control). It causes a **shortage** if set below equilibrium.
- **Price floor**: a legal minimum price (e.g. minimum wage). It causes a **surplus** if set above equilibrium.
- **Question from class**: Why does a binding price ceiling cause a shortage?
  - **Answer**: at the capped price, quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied (Qd > Qs).

**Key terms**
- **Price ceiling**: a legal maximum price (e.g. rent control).
- **Price floor**: a legal minimum price (e.g. minimum wage).

## 7. Consumer Surplus, Producer Surplus, and Total Surplus
- **Consumer surplus (CS)** = willingness to pay − price paid
- **Producer surplus (PS)** = price received − cost
- **Total surplus** = CS + PS
- Total surplus is **maximized at equilibrium**.

**Key terms**
- **Consumer surplus**: willingness to pay minus price paid.
- **Producer surplus**: price received minus cost.
- **Total surplus**: CS + PS, maximized at equilibrium.
