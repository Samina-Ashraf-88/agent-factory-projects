## 1. Causes of the French Revolution
- The French Revolution began in 1789.
- Three causes from the lecture:
  - **Financial crisis**: debt from wars, including the American War of Independence.
  - **Bad harvests**: these pushed bread prices up.
  - **Enlightenment ideas**: from thinkers such as Rousseau and Montesquieu.
- **The professor said the causes will definitely be on the midterm.**

**Key terms**
- **Financial crisis**: a cause of the Revolution, driven by debt from wars including the American War of Independence.
- **Enlightenment ideas**: a cause of the Revolution, linked in the notes to Rousseau and Montesquieu.

## 2. From the Estates-General to the National Assembly (1789)
- The Estates-General met in May 1789. It had not met since 1614.
- It had three estates:
  - **First Estate**: the clergy
  - **Second Estate**: the nobility
  - **Third Estate**: everyone else (about 97% of the population)
- The Third Estate broke away and formed the National Assembly.
- The Tennis Court Oath took place on 20 June 1789.

**Key terms**
- **Estates-General**: an assembly of France's three estates. It met in May 1789 for the first time since 1614.
- **Third Estate**: everyone outside the clergy and nobility, about 97% of the population.
- **National Assembly**: the body the Third Estate formed after breaking away from the Estates-General.
- **Tennis Court Oath**: an event on 20 June 1789, linked to the National Assembly's formation.

## 3. The Bastille and the Declaration of Rights (1789)
- The storming of the Bastille happened on 14 July 1789. That date is now France's national day.
- The Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen came in August 1789. Its principles were liberty, equality and popular sovereignty.

**Key terms**
- **Storming of the Bastille**: an event on 14 July 1789, now marked as France's national day.
- **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen**: an August 1789 declaration setting out liberty, equality and popular sovereignty.

## 4. The Reign of Terror (1793–94)
- The Reign of Terror lasted from 1793 to 1794.
- Key figures: Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety.
- There were about 17,000 official executions.
- Robespierre was executed in July 1794 (Thermidor).

**Key terms**
- **Reign of Terror**: the period from 1793 to 1794, linked to Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety, with about 17,000 official executions.
- **Committee of Public Safety**: named in the notes alongside Robespierre in connection with the Reign of Terror.
- **Thermidor**: the label in the notes for Robespierre's execution in July 1794.

## 5. The End of the Revolution
- Napoleon's coup in 1799 is usually taken as the end of the Revolution.

**Key terms**
- **Napoleon's coup (1799)**: the event usually seen as ending the Revolution.
