# HIST 210 — Week 5: The French Revolution (1789–1799)

## ⭐ Causes (prof: will definitely be on the midterm)
1. **Financial crisis**: France was deep in debt from wars, including its support for the American War of Independence.
2. **Bad harvests**: these pushed bread prices very high and fed popular anger.
3. **Enlightenment ideas**: thinkers like **Rousseau** and **Montesquieu** challenged absolute monarchy and traditional authority.

## Timeline

| Date | Event | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **May 1789** | Estates-General meets | First time since **1614** |
| **20 June 1789** | Tennis Court Oath | The Third Estate breaks away and becomes the **National Assembly** |
| **14 July 1789** | Storming of the Bastille | Now France's national day |
| **Aug 1789** | Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen | Liberty, equality, popular sovereignty |
| **1793–94** | Reign of Terror | Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety; about **17,000** official executions |
| **July 1794** | Robespierre executed | The "Thermidorian Reaction" (named after the revolutionary month Thermidor) |
| **1799** | Napoleon's coup | Usually treated as the end of the Revolution |

## Key concepts

**The Three Estates**
- **First Estate**: clergy
- **Second Estate**: nobility
- **Third Estate**: everyone else, about **97%** of the population

**Key figures**
- **Robespierre**: led the Committee of Public Safety during the Terror and was executed in July 1794
- **Napoleon**: his 1799 coup ended the revolutionary period
- **Rousseau and Montesquieu**: Enlightenment thinkers whose ideas helped cause the Revolution
