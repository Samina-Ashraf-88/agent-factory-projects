# ECON 101: Week 4 Study Notes
**Topics:** Supply & Demand · Equilibrium · Elasticity · Price Controls · Surplus

---

## 1. Demand

**Law of Demand:** As price rises, quantity demanded falls, *ceteris paribus* (all else equal).
→ That's why the **demand curve slopes downward** ⬇️

### Determinants of Demand (these SHIFT the curve)
| Determinant | Example |
|---|---|
| **Income** | See normal vs. inferior goods below |
| **Prices of related goods** | **Substitutes:** price of Pepsi ↑ → demand for Coke ↑ <br> **Complements:** price of printers ↑ → demand for ink ↓ |
| **Tastes** | A trend makes a product popular |
| **Expectations** | Expect the price to rise next month → buy more now |
| **Number of buyers** | Population grows → demand ↑ |

### Normal vs. Inferior Goods
- **Normal good:** income ↑ → demand ↑
- **Inferior good:** income ↑ → demand ↓ (e.g. instant noodles)

---

## 2. Supply

**Law of Supply:** Price ↑ → quantity supplied ↑ (the supply curve slopes **upward**)

### Determinants of Supply (these SHIFT the curve)
- **Input prices:** higher costs → supply ↓
- **Technology:** better tech → supply ↑
- **Expectations:** about future prices
- **Number of sellers:** more sellers → supply ↑

---

## 3. ⚠️ Movement Along vs. Shift of the Curve (VERY IMPORTANT)

| | Caused by | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| **Movement along** the curve | A change in the good's **own price** | "Change in **quantity** demanded/supplied" |
| **Shift** of the curve | A change in a **determinant** | "Change in demand/supply" |

> 💡 **Exam trap:** "A price increase decreases demand" is **wrong**. A price increase decreases **quantity demanded**.

---

## 4. Equilibrium

- **Equilibrium** is where **Qd = Qs**
- The equilibrium price is also called the **market-clearing price**

| Price is... | Result |
|---|---|
| **Above** equilibrium | **Surplus** (Qs > Qd) |
| **Below** equilibrium | **Shortage** (Qd > Qs) |

---

## 5. Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

$$PED = \frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta P}$$

| Value | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| \|PED\| > 1 | **Elastic** | Buyers are very responsive to price |
| \|PED\| < 1 | **Inelastic** | Buyers are not very responsive |
| \|PED\| = 1 | **Unit elastic** | Proportional response |

**Inelastic examples:** insulin, gasoline (in the short run)

### Midpoint Method
$$\%\Delta = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{(\text{new} + \text{old})/2} \times 100$$

**Worked example:** Price rises from $4 → $6 and Qd falls from 120 → 80.
- %ΔP = (6 − 4) / 5 × 100 = **40%**
- %ΔQd = (80 − 120) / 100 × 100 = **−40%**
- PED = −40 / 40 = **−1** → |PED| = 1 → **unit elastic**

> 💡 The midpoint method gives the same answer whether price goes up or down. That's why it's used.

## 6. Price Controls

| | Definition | Example | When binding | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Price ceiling** | Legal **maximum** price | Rent control | Set **below** equilibrium | **Shortage** |
| **Price floor** | Legal **minimum** price | Minimum wage | Set **above** equilibrium | **Surplus** |

> 💡 **Memory trick:** a ceiling is *up high* but only matters when it's *low*. A floor is *down low* but only matters when it's *high*.

**Class Q:** *Why does a binding price ceiling cause a shortage?*
**A:** At the capped price, **Qd > Qs**. The low price makes buyers want more while sellers offer less.

---

## 7. Consumer & Producer Surplus

- **Consumer Surplus (CS)** = willingness to pay − price paid
- **Producer Surplus (PS)** = price received − cost
- **Total Surplus** = CS + PS, which is **maximized at equilibrium**

> 💡 Price controls usually reduce total surplus. This lost surplus is called *deadweight loss* (check whether your class has covered it yet).

## ✅ Quick Self-Test
1. The price of coffee rises. What happens to demand for tea? For cream?
2. Income rises and demand for bus rides falls. What kind of good are bus rides?
3. A frost destroys orange crops. Is this a shift or a movement? Which curve moves?
4. Price falls from $10 to $8 and Qd rises from 100 to 150. Find the PED with the midpoint method. Is demand elastic or inelastic?
5. The government sets a minimum price above equilibrium. Is there a surplus or a shortage?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Tea demand ↑ (substitute); cream demand ↓ (complement)
2. Inferior good
3. Shift: **supply** shifts left (number of sellers / input availability falls)
4. %ΔP = −2/9 ≈ −22.2%; %ΔQd = 50/125 = 40%; PED ≈ −1.8 → **elastic**
5. Surplus (a binding price floor)
</details>
