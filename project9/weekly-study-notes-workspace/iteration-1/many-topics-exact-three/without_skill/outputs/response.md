# ECON 101 — Week 4 Study Notes: Supply, Demand & Elasticity

## 1. Demand

**Law of Demand:** When price rises, quantity demanded falls, *ceteris paribus* (all else held equal).
→ This is why the **demand curve slopes downward**.

### Determinants of demand (these SHIFT the curve)
| Determinant | Example of a shift |
|---|---|
| **Income** | See normal vs. inferior goods below |
| **Prices of related goods** | **Substitutes:** price of Coke ↑ → demand for Pepsi ↑<br>**Complements:** price of printers ↑ → demand for ink ↓ |
| **Tastes** | A product becomes trendy → demand ↑ |
| **Expectations** | Expect prices to rise next month → demand ↑ now |
| **Number of buyers** | More consumers in the market → demand ↑ |

### Normal vs. inferior goods
- **Normal good:** income ↑ → demand ↑
- **Inferior good:** income ↑ → demand ↓ (e.g., instant noodles)

---

## 2. Supply

**Law of Supply:** When price rises, quantity supplied rises.
→ The supply curve slopes **upward**.

### Determinants of supply (these SHIFT the curve)
- **Input prices**: inputs get cheaper → supply ↑
- **Technology**: better technology → supply ↑
- **Expectations** about future prices
- **Number of sellers**: more sellers → supply ↑

---

## 3. ⚠️ Movement Along vs. Shift of the Curve (VERY IMPORTANT)

| | What causes it | What you call it |
|---|---|---|
| **Movement along** the curve | A change in the good's **own price** | Change in **quantity demanded / quantity supplied** |
| **Shift** of the curve | A change in a **determinant** (anything except own price) | Change in **demand / supply** |

**Exam trap:** "The price of coffee rose, so demand for coffee fell" is **wrong**. Correct version: *quantity demanded* fell, which is a movement along the curve.

---

## 4. Equilibrium

- **Equilibrium:** where **Qd = Qs**
- **Equilibrium price** is also called the **market-clearing price**

| Price is... | Result | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Above** equilibrium | **Surplus** | Qs > Qd |
| **Below** equilibrium | **Shortage** | Qd > Qs |

---

## 5. Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

$$PED = \frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta P}$$

PED is usually negative because of the law of demand, so we classify it using the **absolute value**:

| \|PED\| | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| > 1 | **Elastic** | Quantity responds strongly to price |
| < 1 | **Inelastic** | Quantity barely responds to price |
| = 1 | **Unit elastic** | Same proportional change |

**Inelastic examples:** insulin (a necessity with no substitute), gasoline in the **short run**

### Midpoint method
$$\%\Delta = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{(\text{new} + \text{old})/2} \times 100$$

**Worked example:** Price goes from $4 → $6, and Qd goes from 120 → 80.
- %ΔQd = (80 − 120) / 100 × 100 = **−40%**
- %ΔP = (6 − 4) / 5 × 100 = **+40%**
- PED = −40 / 40 = **−1** → |PED| = 1 → **unit elastic**

*Why use the midpoint method?* You get the same answer whether the price goes up or down.

---

## 6. Price Controls

| | Definition | Example | Binding when... | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Price ceiling** | Legal **maximum** price | Rent control | Set **below** equilibrium | **Shortage** |
| **Price floor** | Legal **minimum** price | Minimum wage | Set **above** equilibrium | **Surplus** |

**Q from class:** *Why does a binding price ceiling cause a shortage?*
**A:** At the capped price, **Qd > Qs**. The low price draws in more buyers but discourages sellers.

*Memory tip:* a **ceiling** has to sit **low** (below equilibrium) to matter, and a **floor** has to sit **high** (above equilibrium) to matter.

---

## 7. Consumer & Producer Surplus

- **Consumer surplus (CS)** = willingness to pay − price paid
- **Producer surplus (PS)** = price received − cost
- **Total surplus** = CS + PS
- Total surplus is **maximized at equilibrium**

## ✅ Quick Self-Test (answers below)

1. Income rises and demand for bus rides falls. What kind of good are bus rides?
2. The price of pizza falls. Is this a shift of the pizza demand curve or a movement along it?
3. A new technology lowers production costs. Which curve shifts, and in which direction?
4. Price rises from $10 to $12 and Qd falls from 50 to 40. Use the midpoint method to find PED. Is demand elastic or inelastic?
5. The government sets a minimum price above equilibrium. Is there a shortage or a surplus?
6. A consumer would pay up to $15 for a ticket and pays $9. What is their consumer surplus?

1. An inferior good
2. A movement along the curve (quantity demanded rises)
3. Supply shifts right (supply increases)
4. %ΔQ = −10/45 ≈ −22.2%, %ΔP = 2/11 ≈ 18.2%, so PED ≈ −1.22. |PED| > 1, so demand is **elastic**
5. A surplus (Qs > Qd)
6. $6
