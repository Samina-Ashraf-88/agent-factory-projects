## 1. Demand
- **Law of demand:** as price rises, quantity demanded falls, *ceteris paribus*.
- The demand curve **slopes downward**. This was stressed in the notes, so remember it.
- Determinants of demand (changes in these **shift** the demand curve):
  - Income
  - Prices of related goods (substitutes and complements)
  - Tastes
  - Expectations
  - Number of buyers
- A **normal good** sees demand go up when income goes up.
- An **inferior good** sees demand go down when income goes up (e.g. instant noodles).

**Key terms**
- **Law of demand**: as price rises, quantity demanded falls, ceteris paribus.
- **Determinants of demand**: income, prices of related goods (substitutes, complements), tastes, expectations, number of buyers. A change in any of them shifts the demand curve.
- **Normal good**: a good whose demand goes up when income goes up.
- **Inferior good**: a good whose demand goes down when income goes up (e.g. instant noodles).

## 2. Supply
- **Law of supply:** when price goes up, quantity supplied goes up.
- Determinants of supply (changes in these shift the supply curve):
  - Input prices
  - Technology
  - Expectations
  - Number of sellers

**Key terms**
- **Law of supply**: when price goes up, quantity supplied goes up.
- **Determinants of supply**: input prices, technology, expectations, number of sellers.

## 3. Movement Along vs. Shift of the Curve
- **Movement along a curve** comes from a change in **price**.
- **Shift of a curve** comes from a change in a **determinant**.
- The notes mark this as a **very important distinction**.

**Key terms**
- **Movement along the curve**: caused by a change in price.
- **Shift of the curve**: caused by a change in a determinant (of demand or supply).

## 4. Market Equilibrium
- **Equilibrium** is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied (Qd = Qs).
- The equilibrium price is also called the **market-clearing price**.
- If price is **above** equilibrium, there is a **surplus**.
- If price is **below** equilibrium, there is a **shortage**.

**Key terms**
- **Equilibrium**: the point where Qd = Qs.
- **Market-clearing price**: another name for the equilibrium price.
- **Surplus**: occurs when price is above equilibrium.
- **Shortage**: occurs when price is below equilibrium.

## 5. Price Elasticity of Demand
- **PED = %ΔQd / %ΔP**
- How to classify it (using the absolute value):
  - |PED| > 1 means **elastic**
  - |PED| < 1 means **inelastic**
  - |PED| = 1 means **unit elastic**
- Examples of inelastic goods: insulin, and gasoline in the short run.
- **Midpoint method** for percentage change:
  - %Δ = (new − old) / ((new + old) / 2) × 100

**Key terms**
- **Price elasticity of demand (PED)**: %ΔQd / %ΔP.
- **Elastic**: |PED| > 1.
- **Inelastic**: |PED| < 1 (e.g. insulin, gasoline in the short run).
- **Unit elastic**: |PED| = 1.
- **Midpoint method**: %Δ = (new − old) / ((new + old) / 2) × 100.

## 6. Price Controls
- A **price ceiling** is a legal maximum price (e.g. rent control).
  - If it is set below equilibrium, it causes a **shortage**.
  - Covered in class: a binding price ceiling causes a shortage because at the capped price, **Qd > Qs**.
- A **price floor** is a legal minimum price (e.g. minimum wage).
  - If it is set above equilibrium, it causes a **surplus**.

**Key terms**
- **Price ceiling**: a legal maximum price (e.g. rent control). It causes a shortage if set below equilibrium.
- **Price floor**: a legal minimum price (e.g. minimum wage). It causes a surplus if set above equilibrium.

## 7. Consumer, Producer and Total Surplus
- **Consumer surplus** = willingness to pay − price paid.
- **Producer surplus** = price received − cost.
- **Total surplus** = consumer surplus + producer surplus (CS + PS).
- Total surplus is **maximized at equilibrium**.

**Key terms**
- **Consumer surplus (CS)**: willingness to pay − price paid.
- **Producer surplus (PS)**: price received − cost.
- **Total surplus**: CS + PS, which is maximized at equilibrium.
