# HIST 210 — Week 5 Study Notes

## 1. Causes of the French Revolution
- The French Revolution started in 1789.
- **The professor said the causes will definitely be on the midterm.**
- Main causes:
  - **Financial crisis**: debt from wars, including the American War of Independence.
  - **Bad harvests**, which pushed bread prices up.
  - **Enlightenment ideas**, e.g. Rousseau and Montesquieu.

**Key terms**
- **Financial crisis**: France's debt from wars, including the American War of Independence. One cause of the Revolution.
- **Bad harvests**: poor harvests that raised bread prices. One cause of the Revolution.
- **Enlightenment ideas**: the ideas of thinkers such as Rousseau and Montesquieu. One cause of the Revolution.

## 2. The Estates-General and the National Assembly
- The Estates-General met in May 1789, the first time since 1614.
- It had three estates:
  - First Estate: clergy
  - Second Estate: nobility
  - Third Estate: everyone else (about 97% of the population)
- The Third Estate broke away and formed the National Assembly.
- Tennis Court Oath: 20 June 1789.

**Key terms**
- **Estates-General**: an assembly of the three estates. It met in May 1789 for the first time since 1614.
- **Third Estate**: everyone outside the clergy and nobility, about 97% of the population.
- **National Assembly**: the body the Third Estate formed after breaking away from the Estates-General.
- **Tennis Court Oath**: an oath taken on 20 June 1789, linked to the forming of the National Assembly.

## 3. Key Events of 1789
- **Storming of the Bastille**: 14 July 1789. This date is now France's national day.
- **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen**: August 1789. Its principles were liberty, equality and popular sovereignty.

**Key terms**
- **Storming of the Bastille**: an event on 14 July 1789. The date is now the French national day.
- **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen**: an August 1789 document setting out liberty, equality and popular sovereignty.
- **Popular sovereignty**: one of the principles in the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen.

## 4. The Reign of Terror
- The Reign of Terror lasted from 1793 to 1794.
- Its key figure was Robespierre, along with the Committee of Public Safety.
- There were about 17,000 official executions.
- Robespierre was executed in July 1794 (Thermidor).

**Key terms**
- **Reign of Terror**: the period from 1793 to 1794, linked to Robespierre and the Committee of Public Safety, with about 17,000 official executions.
- **Committee of Public Safety**: a body linked with Robespierre during the Reign of Terror.
- **Thermidor**: July 1794, when Robespierre was executed.

## 5. End of the Revolution
- Napoleon's coup took place in 1799. It is usually seen as the end of the Revolution.

**Key terms**
- **Napoleon's coup (1799)**: the event usually treated as the end of the Revolution.
