---

## ⭐ Causes of the Revolution *(definitely on the midterm)*

| Cause | Key points |
|---|---|
| **1. Financial crisis** | Huge state debt from wars, including French support for the **American War of Independence** |
| **2. Bad harvests** | Poor harvests → **high bread prices** → hunger and popular anger |
| **3. Enlightenment ideas** | Thinkers like **Rousseau** and **Montesquieu** questioned absolute monarchy and traditional authority |

**Memory hook:** **M**oney, **M**eals, **M**inds (debt, bread, ideas)

---

## Timeline of Key Events

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **May 1789** | **Estates-General** meets | First meeting since **1614** |
| **June 1789** | Third Estate breaks away → **National Assembly** | Commoners claim to represent the nation |
| **20 June 1789** | **Tennis Court Oath** | National Assembly vows not to disband until France has a constitution |
| **14 July 1789** | **Storming of the Bastille** | Symbol of revolt against royal power; now **French National Day** |
| **August 1789** | **Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen** | Liberty, equality, popular sovereignty |
| **1793–1794** | **Reign of Terror** | Led by **Robespierre** and the **Committee of Public Safety**; ~**17,000** official executions |
| **July 1794** | **Robespierre executed** (Thermidor) | End of the Terror |
| **1799** | **Napoleon's coup** | Usually treated as the end of the Revolution |

---

## The Three Estates

| Estate | Who | Share of population |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | Clergy | Small minority |
| **Second** | Nobility | Small minority |
| **Third** | Everyone else (peasants, workers, middle class/bourgeoisie) | **~97%** |

---

## Key People and Terms

- **Rousseau, Montesquieu**: Enlightenment thinkers whose ideas shaped the Revolution
- **Robespierre**: leading figure of the Reign of Terror, executed July 1794
- **Committee of Public Safety**: the body that ran the Terror
- **Thermidor**: the July 1794 overthrow of Robespierre (named after a month in the revolutionary calendar)
- **Popular sovereignty**: the idea that power comes from the people, not the king
